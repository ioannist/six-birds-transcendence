\begin{abstract}
Thought experiments about copying, teleporting and uploading ask whether the
result is the same subject. We ask a prior question that can be answered
exactly. When the organization described by a closure architecture for
consciousness is moved to a different finite system, what would make the
result a continuation rather than a copy? We show that the question splits
into three separately checked parts: whether the target realizes the
architecture's conditions, whether the episode passes through handoffs in
which both systems are live with no gap, and whether the target keeps itself
going once the source and every transfer aid are withdrawn. A certificate that
checks the three is sound, finite models pass it, and finite countermodels
show what fails without each part. A detached copy and a gap fail the lineage
check, and success up to any finite horizon does not establish unbounded
maintenance. A public shadow shows that public observations lose information
about the predictive state. Under symmetric fission,
continuation is a relation with two values, and nothing in the structure picks
one continuer. On one designed model, all eight conditions, as adopted at
interfaces fixed in advance, are computed jointly from one execution, and a
weaker continuation, by ancestry, holds for both branches of a fission and for
a guarded fusion once each has completed its own acquisition. Every result is checked in Lean with standard axioms
only. We claim nothing about consciousness, survival, numerical identity or
substrate independence.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords.} continuation, personal identity, fission,
viability, finite models, Lean, mathematical consciousness science.
