\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked what would make the result of moving a closure architecture to a
different finite system a continuation rather than a copy. Our answer has three
parts, each checked separately: realization on the target, strict live-overlap
lineage, and autonomy after withdrawal. A certificate that checks them is
sound, finite models pass it, and finite countermodels show what fails when a
check is dropped or replaced by endpoint agreement. Continuation is a relation,
and under symmetric fission nothing in the structure selects one continuer. A
target inherits its present relative to the route the transfer took. On one
designed model, all eight conditions, as adopted at interfaces fixed in
advance, are computed jointly from one execution. A weaker continuation, by
ancestry, holds there for both branches of a fission and for a guarded fusion,
each after its own acquisition.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
We state them once.
\begin{itemize}
\item Every result is about finite physical state spaces, some with unbounded
histories. We do not generalize to noisy, large or continuous systems.
\item The strict continuation instances meet conditions (4) to (7) only in
simplified form. The model that computes all eight conditions supports only
continuation by ancestry, under two adopted readings. Its certificates sit at
different checkpoints.
\item Autonomy is model-relative. Disturbances are limited to declared
classes, with no robustness to resource shocks, and nothing here establishes
indefinite physical maintenance.
\item Robustness is shown for one narrow box in one extension of one model.
\item Nothing here shows that any measurement of a real system identifies
these structures.
\item Nothing here establishes consciousness, felt continuity, survival,
numerical identity or Substrate Indifference.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Open problems.}
\begin{itemize}
\item Exhibit a model with strict live-overlap lineage and all eight conditions
at design grade, or prove that some stated obstruction prevents one.
\item Prove that a certificate of the architecture is sound for what it is meant
to capture, or give a reason why no such proof is possible.
\item Extend autonomy beyond declared disturbance classes, to robustness
against resource shocks.
\item Find a converse for the continuation theorem: conditions under which a
continuation that realizes the conditions must have a passing certificate.
\end{itemize}
