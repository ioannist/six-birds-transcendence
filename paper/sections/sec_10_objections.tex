\section{Objections and replies}
\label{sec:objections}

We state the strongest objections we know in the objector's own terms, and
answer each.

\paragraph{``This is Parfit's fission case in new notation.''}
The cases are Parfit's \citep{Parfit1984}, and we use them gladly. Here we
pose the structural questions they raise as exact predicates on finite systems
and check them in Lean. Parfit's Branch-Line case contrasts a
replica's qualitative similarity with numerical identity. We set beside it a
different contrast, between endpoint agreement and strict lineage. We do not
identify the two contrasts, and nothing here bears on whether identity is what
matters, on reductionism, or on persons at all.

\paragraph{``A perfect copy is a continuation.''}
Under some criteria it is. Under the lineage check of this paper it is not,
because no stage has source and copy live together
(Proposition~\ref{prop:detached}). We do not claim our criterion is the right
criterion of personal identity, and we do not decide identity by stipulation.
The claim is narrower and exact: endpoint agreement, realization and autonomy
can all hold without the overlap that our lineage check requires. Anyone who
counts the copy as a continuation must then say which check they are
dropping.

\paragraph{``Your systems are tiny, so nothing here scales.''}
The physical state spaces are finite and small, and the histories are
unbounded. Every general result is proved for arbitrary types or arbitrary
finite systems, and only the instances are small. The countermodels need to be
small: one finite counterexample refutes a general shortcut. We do not
generalize the exact results to noisy, large or continuous systems, and doing
so would need its own approximate theory.

\paragraph{``You have shown that consciousness can be uploaded.''}
We have not. Our results concern the structure described by the closure
architecture, moved between finite systems. Whether satisfying that structure
is being conscious is a separate question. The architecture paper answers it
only conditionally, given its realization postulate \citep{TsiokosArchitecture2026}.
Substrate Indifference remains a postulate, and our results do not address it:
moving structure between finite systems of different types is not an
invariance theorem across substrates. The hypotheses linking structure to felt
time and felt continuity are hypotheses, not conclusions of any result.

\paragraph{``The readings you adopted make the ancestry result easy.''}
The ancestry theorem does rest on two adopted readings, and we state them
where it is used (Section~\ref{sec:ancestry}). Each replaces a stronger
requirement that we do not use, for reasons stated where it applies. The first replaces a
requirement on how records of exchanges must end. On the earlier
shared-execution model, no immediate successor of a target is admitted under
it. It asks instead for a completed local
cycle with continuously authenticated residence, plus mandatory links to the
current generator, ownership and control. The second replaces the demand that
a finite state space list every growing history. It uses a finite certificate
instead, lifted to every full history. A reader who rejects either reading
should read Theorem~\ref{thm:ancestry} as conditional on it. The strict
continuation theorem uses neither reading.

\paragraph{``A certificate that passes is not a certificate that is sound.''}
Agreed. Passing our checks shows that the stated structural properties hold.
Whether those properties are sound evidence for anything about consciousness
is an open bridge, and we leave it open.

\paragraph{``Archives survive withdrawal, so the source is not really gone.''}
Correct, and we say so. After withdrawal, the generated records of the
source's history and of the births of its successors can remain. Our results
infer neither erasure of the source's memory nor numerical identity from
withdrawal.

\paragraph{``The record arrow is just a counter.''}
In a sense, yes. The arrow in the temporal signature is an ordering by the
growth of the system's own records, transported and checked as such. It is not
a thermodynamic arrow and not an arrow defined by path reversal, and none of
our results concerns those.

\paragraph{``Then nothing has been shown about the architecture as a whole.''}
Something has, and it is stated exactly. One designed model computes all eight
conditions jointly from one execution, at interfaces fixed in advance and with
stated choices (Section~\ref{sec:ancestry}). That model supports continuation
by ancestry, not strict live-overlap continuation. The models that support
strict continuation meet four conditions only in simplified form. No model
here has both strict lineage and all eight conditions at design grade, and the
architecture's integrated instance remains open.
