# S34 — Overlap transfer that degrades the source

**Battery item:** 34 (supplemental stress control)
**Plan wording:** Source-target coupling improves target fidelity while progressively damaging the source package.
**Cross-carrier/control status:** `asymmetric_cost_cross_carrier_case`
**Outcome vector:** deliberately unfilled (`S-T=UNFILLED; S-R=UNFILLED; S-L=UNFILLED; A=UNFILLED; R=UNFILLED`)
**Machine records:** `scenarios/PROTOCOL_AXIS_MATRIX.csv` and `ledgers/scenario_discriminator_matrix.csv`, keyed by `S34`

## 1. Discriminating purpose

Test whether a candidate tracks asymmetric costs, source-side viability, and whether target success can coexist with source failure during overlap.

## 2. Stipulated scenario facts

- A temporary bridge couples source and target during transfer.
- Each transfer stage improves a declared target fidelity measure but consumes or perturbs source resources.
- The source crosses a viability/package boundary before final target handoff.
- The target may or may not be autonomous after bridge withdrawal; that remains a separate test.

These facts define the case. They do not preassign transport, target-package, lineage, autonomy, recognition, or ontological outputs.

## 7. Scenario-specific stress questions

- Does source failure have any formal effect on target lineage, or only on protocol ethics/cost?
- Can target fidelity be purchased by erasing source predictive information?
- Which ledger records the transfer's asymmetric resource use?
- Does target maintenance continue without the damaged source?

## 10. Blocked inferences

- Source damage is not evidence of target continuation.
- A costly process is not necessarily more identity-preserving.
- Target success does not imply source preservation.
- Source terminal failure does not establish target autonomy.

## 11. Nonclaims

This card does not say whether a conscious subject moves, survives, dies, branches, fuses, or is recreated. It does not assert that the source or target is conscious. It defines an operational stress case for later structural candidates.
