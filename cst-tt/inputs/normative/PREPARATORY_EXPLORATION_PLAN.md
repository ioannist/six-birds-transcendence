# Preparatory Exploration Plan for Consciousness Across Substrates in Six Birds Theory

**Project code:** CST-EXP  
**Date:** 2026-08-09  
**Status:** Plan only; no theorem proving, programming, simulation, or substantive conclusion is authorized by this document.  
**Primary inputs:** the attached Six Birds corpus, the consciousness repository, and the completed 60-paper relevance triage.

---

## 1. Purpose

The purpose of this work is to make the most informed possible choice of a **formal landing target** for a later project about whether, and in what precisely typed sense, a consciousness-bearing closure package could transcend one substrate or medium and continue on another.

This preparatory program is not intended to prove that such continuation is possible, impossible, unique, or empirically recognizable. It is intended to:

1. extract all relevant mathematical and conceptual assets from the attached corpus;
2. expose the exact gaps between fixed-support SBT results and genuinely cross-carrier claims;
3. construct a broad, non-premature design space of candidate objects, invariants, bridges, and continuity criteria;
4. attack those candidates with copy, fission, fusion, destruction, hidden-state, route-holonomy, temporal, autonomy, and approximation counterexamples;
5. compare the surviving candidates with relevant external work and objections;
6. determine which candidate can plausibly support exact finite theorems and small toy programs; and
7. freeze one primary target and at least one fallback only at the end of the exploration.

The output should be a **target-selection dossier**, not a transfer theory and not a transfer protocol.

---

## 2. The central discipline: keep four questions separate

Every exploratory claim must be assigned to exactly one of the following layers, or explicitly state that it links two layers.

### 2.1 Structural transport

Does some mathematically specified object, relation, quotient, process, closure package, or certificate transport from a source carrier to a distinct target carrier under a declared bridge?

This is the layer on which unconditional finite theorems are most likely to be possible.

### 2.2 Target realization

Does the target carrier instantiate the full or relevant part of the C-package—C1 objecthood, C2 strict promotion, C3 reflexive self-coordinate, C4 hiddenness, C5 action credit/mineness, C6 temporal closure, C7 binding, and C8 viability—rather than merely reproduce a public shadow?

A successful structural transport need not answer this automatically.

### 2.3 Personal lineage or continuation

Is the target a continuation of the source subject, a successor, a new branch, a duplicate, a fusion product, or something for which the declared structure gives no unique verdict?

This must not be collapsed into either target realization or public equivalence.

### 2.4 Ontological or phenomenal reading

What additional assumptions would license the language of consciousness, survival, realization rather than simulation, or token-independence? In particular, what work is being done by A1, A2, certificate soundness, or any narrower replacement assumption?

No Tier-O conclusion may be used as a premise in a Tier-S exploration.

A fifth cross-cutting question must also remain visible throughout:

### 2.5 Post-handoff autonomy

After the transfer bridge, source carrier, decoder, or external protocol is removed, does the target maintain the relevant package endogenously? A target that survives only as a readout of the source or as a permanently protocol-supported shadow is not automatically a new-carrier realization.

---

## 3. Non-goals and prohibited shortcuts

The exploratory program must not:

- assume A2 is already a carrier-invariance theorem;
- treat carrier-polymorphic definitions as proof of cross-carrier transport;
- promote fixed-support results to cross-medium results without a new typed bridge;
- identify matched behavior, reports, present macrostates, or input-output behavior with matched hidden or predictive organization;
- use “same consciousness,” “transfer,” “continuity,” or “copy” as unanalysed primitives;
- choose gradual replacement, destructive upload, or any other scenario as paradigmatic before the scenario atlas is complete;
- require a positive result—the strongest defensible landing may be a no-go theorem, an underdetermination theorem, or a conditional positive result paired with a no-go boundary;
- implement Python toys, write Lean proofs, run a full Six Birds simulation, or draft the eventual paper during the exploratory phases;
- use *To Chat a Stone with Six Birds* as a source while the existing owner instruction excluding it remains in force;
- count papers 42 and 60 as independent evidence; they are versions of one Strict Audited Utility research line.

---

## 4. What the exploration must ultimately deliver

The final dossier must make it possible to begin a later theorem-and-toy project without reopening foundational ambiguity. It must contain:

1. a canonical vocabulary and typed neutral problem statement;
2. a source-traceable atlas of relevant corpus assets;
3. a scenario and outcome taxonomy;
4. an atlas of candidate transported objects and invariants;
5. a cross-carrier bridge design space;
6. a counterexample and no-go catalogue;
7. an assumption ladder separating structural, realization, lineage, and ontological claims;
8. two to four surviving candidate targets with an explicit comparison;
9. one frozen primary target and one fallback target;
10. a theorem prospectus containing conjecture-level statements only;
11. a toy-program prospectus containing specifications only;
12. explicit kill criteria, nonclaims, and reasons for rejecting alternatives; and
13. a later implementation handoff describing what would be proved or programmed, without doing that work.

The preferred final shape is not necessarily one headline theorem. The exploration should test whether the strongest landing is a compact package consisting of:

- one core cross-carrier structural theorem;
- one companion no-go or non-uniqueness theorem;
- a finite witness/control suite;
- an approximate or defect-bounded extension if justified; and
- a separately priced conditional consciousness/identity corollary.

This shape is a methodological preference, not a conclusion.

# Phase CST-EXP-00 — Charter, typing discipline, and question decomposition

## Objective

Create a neutral language in which the project can be explored without silently assuming the desired result.

## Work

### A. Freeze scope

Write a one-page charter stating that the object of inquiry is cross-carrier continuation of a consciousness-relevant SBT package, while leaving open:

- whether the transported item is a state, process, quotient, relation, lineage, certificate, or closure-generated object;
- whether continuity is functional, predictive, causal, dynamical, historical, relation-valued, or ontological;
- whether a successful result is exact, approximate, conditional, constructive, or negative.

### B. Canonicalize the core nouns

Define provisional, noncommittal meanings for:

- substrate, medium, carrier, support, implementation, realization;
- source package, target package, bridge, route, handoff, overlap, translation, continuation;
- public interface, predictive interface, hidden state, certificate, audit, admissibility;
- identity, lineage, successor, duplicate, branch, fusion, extinction, dormant interval;
- destructive, nondestructive, gradual, abrupt, reversible, distributed, autonomous;
- exact preservation, approximate preservation, defect, residual, repair.

Each definition must include a “not to be confused with” field.

### C. Establish the claim-type ledger

Every later statement receives one of these tags:

- **S-T:** structural transport;
- **S-R:** target realization structure;
- **S-L:** lineage/continuity structure;
- **A:** autonomy after bridge removal;
- **R:** recognition or empirical correspondence;
- **O:** ontological/phenomenal reading;
- **M:** methodological or analogy-only.

A claim may carry more than one tag only if the connecting implication is explicit.

### D. Write the anti-conflation checklist

At minimum, the checklist must block:

- fixed support → distinct carrier;
- state matching → process continuation;
- public matching → predictive matching;
- predictive matching → full C-package preservation;
- target consciousness → source-target identity;
- exact copying → unique continuation;
- carrier-neutral syntax → substrate-indifference theorem;
- bridge-supported behavior → autonomous target realization;
- local pairwise consistency → global coherent identity package;
- route existence → route independence;
- certificate satisfaction → certificate soundness;
- structural theorem → phenomenal conclusion.

### E. Define the exploration questions

Prepare the question bank that every candidate must answer, including:

1. What exactly is transported?
2. On what mathematical carrier does that object live?
3. What constitutes a bridge between distinct carriers?
4. Is the bridge a function, relation, span, simulation, common refinement, or process?
5. What must be preserved exactly, and what may vary?
6. Does order or path matter?
7. What happens under copying, fission, fusion, and deletion?
8. What happens when the bridge is removed?
9. Can the criterion be checked without direct access to hidden state?
10. Is the criterion compositional across multiple substrate changes?
11. What is the approximate or noisy version?
12. Which conclusion, if any, requires A1, A2, or certificate soundness?

- `ledgers/anti_conflation_checklist.csv`

## Exit gate

No term central to the project may remain defined by the conclusion it is supposed to establish. The four principal questions—transport, realization, lineage, and ontology—must be independently stateable.

# Phase CST-EXP-02 — Scenario atlas and neutral transfer semantics

## Objective

Map the entire problem space before proposing a criterion. The phase should reveal which apparent disagreements are actually disagreements about different transfer protocols or different meanings of continuity.

## Work

### A. Define the neutral source–target frame

Without selecting a final formalism, describe a generic case with:

- a source carrier and source package;
- a distinct target carrier and candidate target package;
- declared source and target interfaces;
- a route or bridge that may have internal stages;
- source and target histories and continuation families;
- an optional period of support overlap;
- a handoff condition;
- a post-handoff autonomy test;
- possible branching or merging.

This frame is only a bookkeeping schema. It must not presuppose that the source and target share an identity object.

### B. Build the protocol-axis matrix

Classify scenarios along independent axes:

- **carrier relation:** same support/re-encoding, partially overlapping support, disjoint support;
- **change mode:** gradual replacement, abrupt translation, checkpoint/restart, live migration, destructive scan, nondestructive copy, distributed extension;
- **source fate:** persists, degrades, is paused, is erased before activation, is erased after activation, remains coupled;
- **target timing:** continuous overlap, delayed activation, temporal gap, time-rescaled activation;
- **branching:** one-to-one, one-to-many, many-to-one, many-to-many;
- **bridge dependence:** permanent, temporary, removable, externally decoded;
- **fidelity:** exact, lossy, error-corrected, residual-bounded, adaptive;
- **reversibility:** reversible, approximately reversible, irreversible;
- **route multiplicity:** unique route, multiple equivalent routes, holonomy-bearing routes;
- **granularity:** microstate, predictive state, package state, event package, certificate only.

### C. Define outcome labels without metaphysical loading

At minimum:

- no valid target package;
- target package exists but no declared lineage relation reaches it;
- structural successor;
- one-to-one lineage continuation;
- branch-valued continuation;
- fusion-valued continuation;
- public duplicate with hidden mismatch;
- package-equivalent duplicate with unresolved token identity;
- bridge-dependent shadow;
- autonomous target realization;
- indeterminate under the declared instruments;
- empty target after viability failure.

These labels must be treated as outputs of candidate criteria, not facts about the scenarios.

### D. Construct a canonical thought-experiment battery

The battery should include at least:

1. gradual neuron-by-neuron or component-by-component replacement;
2. instantaneous destructive scan and reconstruction;
3. nondestructive exact copy while the source continues;
4. copy first, source deletion later;
5. two simultaneous exact targets;
6. target activation after a temporal gap;
7. transfer through a long-lived source-target overlap;
8. distributed extension in which no sharp handoff exists;
9. fission followed by independent divergence;
10. fission followed by later fusion;
11. public/report-perfect copy with predictive mismatch;
12. predictive match with different binding or action-credit structure;
13. target maintained only by an external decoder or source process;
14. route loop that restores every current observable while changing predictive state;
15. local pairwise matches that fail to glue into one global identity package;
16. transfer near a viability boundary;
17. lossy transfer followed by endogenous repair;
18. reversible round trip across substrates;
19. target running at a radically rescaled clock rate;
20. a pure representation/gauge change that should not count as movement to a new carrier.

### E. Extract discriminating questions

For each scenario, identify which features could distinguish candidate verdicts:

- continuity of predictive fibers;
- preservation of admissible continuation structure;
- temporal overlap and causal coupling;
- survival of recombination content;
- ledger and viability continuity;
- source-target action-credit history;
- route holonomy;
- autonomous closure after bridge removal;
- exact versus approximate defect;
- uniqueness or branching of the continuation relation.

## Outputs

- `scenarios/NEUTRAL_TRANSFER_SCHEMA.md`
- `scenarios/PROTOCOL_AXIS_MATRIX.csv`
- `scenarios/OUTCOME_TAXONOMY.md`
- `scenarios/CANONICAL_BATTERY.md`
- one card per canonical scenario;
- `ledgers/scenario_discriminator_matrix.csv`.

## Exit gate

Every later candidate must be capable of returning a typed verdict—or an explicit “underdetermined”—for every scenario in the battery. No single scenario may define the meaning of transfer for the rest.

# Phase CST-EXP-03 — Independent candidate generation and cross-carrier bridge design

## Objective

Generate a wide set of plausible formal targets without converging too early. This phase is deliberately expansive and should preserve incompatible possibilities long enough for genuine comparison.

## Work

### A. Mine candidate transported objects

Explore, separately and in combinations, whether the relevant transported item might be:

- a full C1–C8 certificate package;
- the terminal predictive quotient or its lineage;
- a transition system on predictive classes;
- a continuation algebra or future-sufficient behavior;
- a closure-generated fixed point;
- a viability kernel plus endogenous maintenance law;
- a reflexive self-coordinate and its update structure;
- a temporal closure triple and ledger history;
- recombination information not reducible to branchwise summaries;
- an action-credit/mineness ledger;
- a quality-space geometry or gauge-equivalence class;
- an event package or globally glued object;
- a relation among histories rather than an object at one time;
- an error-corrected logical package distributed across changing support;
- a minimal sufficient bundle of several of the above.

For each candidate object, ask whether it is intrinsic, interface-relative, protocol-relative, history-dependent, or maintained.

### B. Generate candidate invariants

Possible families to investigate—not adopt—include:

- isomorphism of predictive quotients;
- conjugacy or bisimulation of predictive dynamics;
- preservation of all admissible continuation tests;
- preservation of a closure operator and its fixed-point object;
- preservation of recombination/refinement structure;
- preservation of viability and endogenous repair;
- preservation of route-holonomy class;
- preservation of a typed C-package certificate;
- preservation up to gauge or representation equivalence;
- a defect-bounded or residual-bounded approximate invariant;
- a relation-valued lineage invariant allowing fission and fusion.

Each invariant card must assess necessity, sufficiency, compositionality, route sensitivity, copy behavior, approximation, observability, and dependence on hidden state.

### C. Generate candidate bridge types

Explore at least these cross-carrier bridge morphologies:

- direct map between source and target states;
- relation between source and target histories;
- forward simulation;
- bisimulation;
- span through a common abstract package;
- cospan into a common completion;
- source-target overlap process with a moving support boundary;
- continuation functor between event/continuation structures;
- bridge plus repair/recovery operator, analogous to a logical-object migration;
- staged bridge with exactification, promotion, and target autonomy gates;
- approximate bridge carrying an explicit adequacy residual;
- branch-valued relation rather than a function.

The point is to discover which bridge notion makes the desired questions well-typed, not to select the most sophisticated formalism.

### D. Run independent brainstorming lenses

Produce separate memos from at least six perspectives before synthesis:

1. **quotient/algebraic:** identity as predictive quotient and continuation maps;
2. **dynamical/process:** identity as an ongoing causally coupled process rather than a matched state;
3. **error-correcting/logical-object:** persistence of a higher-layer object over changing physical support;
4. **closure/realization:** preservation of the full promoted package and its audit gates;
5. **viability/agent:** maintained self-production, action credit, and autonomy;
6. **branching/philosophical:** relation-valued survival under fission, fusion, and duplication.

A seventh “wild” memo may propose unconventional combinations, but every idea must later be translated into the typed vocabulary or rejected.

### E. Assemble candidate target-schema cards

Each schema card must contain:

- informal one-sentence idea;
- formal objects and types;
- source, target, and bridge data;
- proposed invariant or relation;
- exact and approximate versions;
- expected verdicts on the canonical battery;
- necessary assumptions;
- likely theorem shapes;
- likely counterexamples;
- why it is not merely behavioral copying;
- what it says about fission/fusion;
- what target autonomy means;
- what remains ontological rather than structural.

Generate enough genuinely different schemas to expose the design space; six to ten is a reasonable working range, but conceptual diversity matters more than count.

# Phase CST-EXP-04 — Adversarial stress, no-go mining, and external confrontation

## Objective

Try to break every candidate. The goal is not to rescue favored ideas but to expose hidden assumptions, trivializations, and impossibility boundaries.

## Work

### A. Apply the internal SBT counterexample machinery

Use the following corpus assets as adversarial lenses:

- hiddenness and current-versus-predictive mismatch;
- route holonomy and memory;
- marking, erasure, and recombination;
- local consistency versus global obstruction;
- protocol-trap and fake-arrow controls;
- no-overreading and matched-shadow limits;
- promotion honesty and representative-selected packages;
- temporal closure and noncanonical “now” transport;
- viability collapse and empty targets;
- confluence and critical-pair failure;
- adequacy residuals and blind spots;
- object-persistence relation laws;
- no-go theorems for audited emergence.

### B. Force candidate verdicts on hard cases

Every candidate must be evaluated on at least these adversarial pairs:

1. same public behavior, different predictive quotient;
2. same predictive quotient, different binding structure;
3. same predictive dynamics, different viability/autonomy;
4. exact target copy while source persists;
5. two exact targets with symmetric histories;
6. destructive copy with no causal overlap;
7. gradual replacement with continuous overlap;
8. route-equivalent endpoints reached by holonomically different paths;
9. branchwise matches without recombination preservation;
10. fission with both branches later claiming unique identity;
11. fusion of two previously independent lineages;
12. locally admissible bridges with no global gluing;
13. target dependent forever on an external decoder;
14. approximate transfer whose residual is small globally but catastrophic locally;
15. temporary viability failure followed by reconstruction;
16. gauge-equivalent representation change mistaken for substrate migration;
17. clock discontinuity or temporal gap;
18. bridge composition that gives a different verdict from the direct bridge.

### C. Search for no-go theorem shapes

Without proving them, formulate precise conjectures such as:

- public-shadow data alone cannot determine cross-carrier lineage;
- state isomorphism alone cannot select a unique continuation under symmetric duplication;
- preservation of a predictive quotient without continuation structure is insufficient;
- branchwise preservation does not imply recombination or global-package preservation;
- a permanently protocol-supported target does not satisfy an intrinsic-autonomy criterion;
- route-independent identity transport requires a flatness or confluence condition not supplied generically;
- approximate global adequacy does not protect viability-critical local structure;
- no criterion invariant under exact duplication can simultaneously award unique continuation to both symmetric copies without becoming relation-valued or adding asymmetry.

These are candidate no-go shapes, not established results.

### D. Maintain a rejection ledger

Candidates should be rejected, narrowed, or split when they:

- merely restate the conclusion;
- are only fixed-support;
- collapse into public equivalence;
- cannot distinguish a target from an external shadow;
- silently spend A2;
- give incoherent fission/fusion verdicts;
- fail bridge composition;
- depend on arbitrary representatives;
- have no finite exact semantics;
- are vacuous because every target can satisfy them;
- are so strong that only identity maps can satisfy them;
- cannot state a falsifier;
- conflict with the C-package architecture or known no-go results.

Rejections must preserve the strongest salvageable subclaim.

### E. Conduct a targeted external-literature challenge pass

Only after internal candidates exist, survey current work relevant to:

- psychological and branching accounts of personal identity;
- mind uploading and whole-brain emulation;
- computational or organizational invariance;
- causal abstraction, implementation, and dynamical equivalence;
- embodiment, enactivism, and process continuity;
- integrated or causal-structure approaches to consciousness;
- logical-object persistence in error correction;
- live process migration, checkpoint/restart, and distributed-system identity;
- formal treatments of copying, continuity, and observer duplication.

The purpose is not to outsource the target. It is to identify prior art, standard objections, unrecognized assumptions, terminology collisions, and decisive counterexamples. Search mainstream journals and relevant preprint servers current to the execution date.

### F. Run explicit red-team positions

Write short adversarial reviews from at least these standpoints:

- strict functionalist: why is exact organization not already enough?
- biological or dynamical continuity critic: why should a translated structure count as continuation?
- duplication critic: how can uniqueness survive symmetric copies?
- SBT audit critic: where is fixed support being smuggled across carriers?
- empirical critic: what could ever distinguish the candidates?
- formal-methods critic: are the objects and bridges actually compositional and well-defined?
- ontological skeptic: which conclusion is purchased rather than proved?

# Phase CST-EXP-05 — Theorem and toy-program reconnaissance

## Objective

Determine which surviving candidate can actually be landed with exact finite mathematics and small programs, without yet writing proofs or code.

## Work

### A. Design minimal finite model families

For each survivor, specify the smallest model capable of distinguishing it from alternatives. The model suite should likely include:

1. **Cross-carrier predictive automata:** two disjoint finite carriers with declared public and future-predictive quotients;
2. **Gradual handoff model:** overlapping source-target support with a moving support boundary;
3. **Destructive copy model:** exact structural translation without causal overlap;
4. **Symmetric duplication model:** one source and two exactly matched targets;
5. **Hidden-counterfeit model:** public/report match with predictive or certificate mismatch;
6. **Fission/fusion and recombination model:** branchwise preservation versus joint recombination information;
7. **Autonomy-removal model:** remove the bridge or decoder and recompute package survival;
8. **Route-holonomy model:** same endpoints, different migration paths, different predictive transport;
9. **Residual-bounded model:** exact versus approximate bridge with local and global adequacy defects;
10. **Viability-threshold model:** transfer near, across, and away from a maintained closure boundary.

Each model card must list states, maps, interfaces, expected witnesses, controls, and which candidates it separates.

### B. Specify later toy programs

No program is written in this phase. Prepare contracts for tools such as:

- a finite quotient and predictive-equivalence calculator;
- a cross-carrier simulation/bisimulation checker;
- a continuation-relation and branch classifier;
- a bridge-composition and confluence checker;
- a route-holonomy finder;
- a recombination-factorization tester;
- a C-package clause-preservation auditor;
- a bridge-removal/autonomy checker;
- an adequacy-residual and blind-spot calculator;
- a small counterexample enumerator over finite carriers.

Each contract must specify input types, exact outputs, witness format, controls, expected complexity, and what result would falsify the candidate.

### C. Formulate theorem families at conjecture grade

For each survivor, write candidate statements in a six-part form: setup, conjecture, proof spine, finite cases, interactions, nonclaims. Candidate families to compare include:

- well-defined cross-carrier predictive transport;
- composition of transport bridges;
- route-independence under a flatness/confluence condition;
- no-go for snapshot or public-shadow copying;
- relation-valued continuation under fission and fusion;
- autonomous-handoff preservation;
- certificate-preservation under typed local obligations;
- approximate stability under residual bounds;
- counterexamples showing failure when each obligation is removed.

Do not mark any statement as proved or theorem-grade.

### D. Assess formalization tractability

For each candidate statement, record:

- required mathematical structures;
- whether finite types, relations, partitions, graphs, and exact rationals suffice;
- whether quotient/setoid complexity is manageable;
- whether category-theoretic language is explanatory or actually necessary;
- dependence on unformalized imported results;
- likely Lean kernel versus Python-enumeration division;
- risk of formalizing a definition that already assumes the result.

Prefer elementary finite formulations for the first landing unless a more abstract form is indispensable.

### E. Build the assumption ladder

For each candidate, state separately:

1. what is unconditional structural mathematics;
2. what establishes target C-package preservation;
3. what establishes lineage rather than mere similarity;
4. what establishes target autonomy;
5. what certificate-soundness or recognition assumption is needed;
6. what A1 licenses;
7. what A2 licenses, and whether a narrower bridge-specific postulate could replace it;
8. which phenomenal or survival language remains interpretive.

# Phase CST-EXP-06 — Synthesis, target freeze, and landing brief

## Objective

Select the most defensible target only after all extraction, generation, and adversarial work is complete.

## Work

### A. Score the finalists

Use hard gates and soft criteria.

**Hard gates—failure disqualifies the target:**

- genuinely cross-carrier typed statement;
- not reducible to public or behavioral matching;
- explicit verdict on duplication and fission;
- target-autonomy condition or explicit reason it is out of scope;
- no hidden use of A2 or the desired identity conclusion;
- source-traceable SBT machinery;
- exact finite semantics;
- positive and negative controls;
- clear nonclaims and falsifiers.

**Soft criteria—score comparatively:**

- explanatory reach across the scenario atlas;
- theorem novelty;
- mathematical cleanliness;
- compositionality;
- treatment of route dependence;
- robustness to approximation;
- faithfulness to the consciousness C-package;
- economy of new primitives;
- compatibility with existing Lean/toy assets;
- clarity of the structural/ontological boundary;
- relation to external literature;
- likelihood that a small theorem-and-toy project can finish cleanly.

### B. Compare rather than merely rank

For every finalist, write:

- strongest case for it;
- strongest counterexample against it;
- unique insight it captures;
- scenario where it outperforms the others;
- assumptions it spends;
- theorem and toy burden;
- what would cause it to be abandoned during implementation.

### C. Freeze a primary and fallback target

The primary target should be the narrowest nontrivial claim that captures the project’s core insight and survives the adversarial battery. The fallback should preserve the most valuable result if the primary’s strongest bridge or sufficiency claim fails—often a no-go, non-uniqueness, or conditional theorem.

No target is frozen merely because it supports the desired philosophical conclusion.

### D. Produce the target specification

The frozen target specification must include:

- one-paragraph motivation;
- exact primitive list and type signatures;
- source, target, bridge, route, and autonomy data;
- formal definition of the candidate continuity/transport relation;
- core conjecture or theorem family;
- companion no-go or boundary result;
- exact finite witness suite;
- toy-program specifications;
- Lean kernel targets;
- dependency map to corpus results;
- assumptions and claim tiers;
- kill criteria;
- excluded claims;
- fallback route.

### E. Conduct a final “could this just be copying?” audit

Before freeze, answer in explicit prose and formal terms:

- what differs between the primary target and exact duplication;
- whether the criterion is relation-valued under symmetric fission;
- whether any uniqueness claim is structural or purchased;
- whether a destructive transfer can satisfy the criterion and under what assumptions;
- whether gradual replacement receives its verdict for substantive reasons rather than intuition;
- whether post-handoff autonomy is demonstrated or assumed;
- whether the result concerns consciousness, identity, both, or only a structural precursor.

### F. Write the later implementation handoff

This is not an implementation plan in detail. It should simply define the next project’s boundaries:

- theorem statements to attempt;
- toy programs to build;
- proof/program order;
- controls required before a positive claim;
- conditions for switching to the fallback target;
- no need for a full Six Birds simulation.

- which counterexamples it survives;

- which toy programs would discriminate it;

No theorem or toy implementation begins before this gate.

The following ledgers should remain live from the first phase to the last.

### 6.1 Provenance ledger

### 6.2 Fixed-support warning ledger

Prevents structural, recognition, and ontological statements from being merged in synthesis prose.

## 7. Decision rules for the final target

The exploration should prefer a target that is:

- **precise:** defined on finite typed structures;
- **cross-carrier:** not a relabeling of fixed-support promotion;
- **discriminating:** separates continuation from at least one counterfeit or duplicate case;
- **branch-aware:** does not force a one-to-one identity function where the mathematics yields a relation;
- **process-aware:** does not rely only on endpoint state matching;
- **closure-aware:** includes target promotion, viability, or autonomy rather than only translation;
- **audit-ready:** has exact witnesses, controls, and failure certificates;
- **assumption-honest:** separates theorem from A1/A2 readings;
- **tractable:** admits small exact toys and Lean-sized kernels;
- **interesting even if negative:** a failed positive target should leave a publishable structural boundary.

A candidate should not be preferred merely because it aligns with intuitive judgments about teleporters or gradual replacement. The scenario judgments are outputs to be explained, not premises to be encoded.

Execute in order:

## 9. Completion criterion for the preparatory program

The preparatory program is complete when it has produced an informed, auditable answer to **what we should try to prove and exhibit next**, while still declining to answer **whether consciousness actually survives a substrate change**.

A successful dossier will make one of the following outcomes explicit:

1. a constructive cross-carrier target appears plausible and survives the full battery;
2. only a conditional target survives, with the missing assumption cleanly exposed;
3. a relation-valued or branch-valued target replaces an untenable unique-identity target;
4. a no-go or underdetermination theorem is the strongest defensible landing;
5. the full C-package target is too strong, but a smaller precursor theorem is both nontrivial and useful.

Any of these is a valid exploration result. The invalid result is a rhetorically attractive target chosen before the corpus, scenarios, counterexamples, and assumptions have been made explicit.
