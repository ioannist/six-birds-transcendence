# CST-EXP-04 Survivor Shortlist

**Status:** three unranked candidate families survive to theorem/toy reconnaissance. They answer different questions and are not interchangeable. The three families remain unranked throughout CST-EXP-05 reconnaissance.

## SF-01 — Integrated Autonomous Handoff

- **Core:** `TS-02` — Full C-Package Staged Handoff
- **Mandatory modules:** `TS-04;TS-06;TS-07;TS-08;TS-09`
- **Optional bridge mechanism:** `TS-05`
- **Claim ceiling:** A heterogeneous episode can establish target-native integrated structural realization and autonomy under explicit package, gluing, route, provenance, residual, and withdrawal conditions.
- **Lineage policy:** No source-target personal identity follows. Lineage must be supplied separately by SF-02 or SF-03, or left underdetermined.
- **Why it survives:** It is nontrivial, cross-carrier typed, blocks public-shadow and decoder counterfeits, and has exact finite failure conditions.
- **Principal risk:** The integrated package may be unrealizable or circular, and certificate soundness remains external.
- **Kill condition:** No finite nontrivial heterogeneous model satisfies the integrated gates, or the package definition presupposes the desired consciousness conclusion.

## SF-02 — Causal-Overlap Process Lineage

- **Core:** `TS-03` — Causal-Overlap Process Continuation
- **Mandatory modules:** `TS-04;TS-07;TS-08`
- **Optional bridge mechanism:** `TS-05`
- **Claim ceiling:** A source and target episode stand in a conservative structural-lineage relation when one noncircular causally connected process moves across support and remains target-autonomous.
- **Lineage policy:** Rejects detached destructive copying and unlicensed temporal gaps; permits relation-valued fission only when the process law does.
- **Why it survives:** Its negative verdicts are principled, testable, and not derived from public equivalence or a same-subject premise.
- **Principal risk:** The process invariant may smuggle biological continuity or be too coarse to protect the C-package.
- **Kill condition:** Every proposed process invariant either reduces to endpoint equivalence, presupposes identity, or makes composition/confluence impossible.

## SF-03 — Relation-Valued Predictive Ancestry

- **Core:** `TS-10` — Heterogeneous Predictive-Ancestry Relation
- **Mandatory modules:** `TS-01;TS-07;TS-09`
- **Optional bridge mechanism:** `TS-05`
- **Claim ceiling:** A heterogeneous ancestry relation can connect histories across carriers while representing fission, fusion, empty targets, route dependence, and bounded predictive defects.
- **Lineage policy:** Continuation is explicitly relation-valued; symmetric copies may both be successors, and no unique numerical identity is asserted.
- **Why it survives:** It gives coherent duplication/fission/fusion verdicts without arbitrary tie-breaking and has finite relational semantics.
- **Principal risk:** The ancestry generator can become permissive, circular, or detached from integrated realization and autonomy.
- **Kill condition:** No finite generator separates genuine ancestry from independently constructed twins without adding a conclusion-equivalent primitive.

## Shared admission conditions

A family survives only because it (1) is genuinely cross-carrier typed, (2) has finite falsifiers, (3) returns relation-valued or underdetermined outputs under symmetry rather than arbitrary uniqueness, (4) exposes route and autonomy assumptions, and (5) does not use A2-P or a same-subject premise to pass.

## Not shortlisted as standalone families

`TS-01`, `TS-04`, `TS-05`, `TS-06`, `TS-07`, `TS-08`, and `TS-09` remain scientifically active as components. Their narrowing is not deletion: each supplies a test that one or more survivors must pass.

CST-EXP-05 must determine which family—or which narrow theorem/no-go inside a family—has the smallest exact finite semantics and most informative toy-program contract. No primary target is frozen here.
