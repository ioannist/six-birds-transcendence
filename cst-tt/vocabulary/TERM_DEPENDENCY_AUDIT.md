# Term dependency and circularity audit

**Audit objective:** verify the CST-EXP-00 exit condition that no central term is defined by the conclusion it is meant to establish.

## 1. Dependency layers

### Layer P — declared primitives and placeholders

The project begins with separately declared types and relations, not with consciousness transfer:

- carrier types and their state/action or history structure;
- package types and package instances;
- time or order domains;
- interfaces and admissible test families;
- relations, maps, kernels, spans, or other candidate bridge shapes;
- interventions and resource-removal operations;
- equality, equivalence, order, metrics, and defect predicates supplied by a candidate.

These are ordinary mathematical inputs. CST-EXP-00 does not assert that any one selection is the correct consciousness ontology.

### Layer S — derived structural terms

Support, bridge, route, handoff, transport, preservation, defect, viability, certificate, continuation, lineage, branch, fusion, extinction, and autonomy are defined from Layer-P data. None is defined by “the same subject survives.”

### Layer R — recognition terms

Recognition bridge and empirical correspondence relate accessible evidence to structural predicates. They do not define those predicates and cannot establish ontology by themselves.

### Layer O — ontological terms

Consciousness, subjecthood, personal survival, and ontological realization are not given unconditional project-local definitions. They appear only as consequents of explicitly tagged `CST:O` conditionals under named premises such as certificate soundness, A1, A2, or narrower alternatives.

## 2. Circularity test

For each central term, ask:

1. Can the term be evaluated without first deciding whether consciousness transferred?
2. Are its inputs typed independently of its desired verdict?
3. Does it distinguish positive, negative, branching, and underdetermined cases?
4. Does its “not to be confused with” field block the nearest illicit conclusion?
5. Could two rival candidates instantiate the term differently without changing the definition midstream?

A term fails if its definition contains “the thing that preserves the same consciousness,” “the true continuer,” or another conclusion-selecting phrase.

## 4. Independent stateability audit

The five principal questions can be stated with different predicates over the same typed data:

- `Transport_Φ(B,p_s,p_t)` — structural transport;
- `TargetPackage_C(X_t,p_t,I_t)` — target realization structure;
- `L_B(p_s,p_t)` — lineage;
- `Aut_W(p_t;Φ,Δ)` — post-handoff autonomy;
- `Recognized_J(...)` and `Ontic_P(...)` — recognition and ontological readings.

None occurs inside the definition of another. Cross-tag implications are permitted only as separately stated claims with premises.

## 5. Known residual ambiguities retained intentionally

The audit does **not** settle:

- the correct individuation rule for package tokens;
- the correct substrate classifier;
- the correct bridge type;
- whether the transported object should be a predictive quotient, a full closure package, a process, a relation, or another object;
- whether a dormant interval can preserve lineage;
- whether route holonomy should count as trauma, rewrite, or failure;
- whether any structural criterion supports personal identity.

Leaving these open is not a vocabulary defect. Selecting answers now would violate the target-neutral charter.

## 6. Audit verdict

**PASS.** All central project terms are reducible to declared mathematical, implementation, intervention, evidential, or postulate data without presupposing successful consciousness transfer. Interpretive terms remain tagged and conditional. The glossary amendment rule is required to preserve this result in later phases.
