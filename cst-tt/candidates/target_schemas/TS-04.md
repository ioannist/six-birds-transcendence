# TS-04 — Viability–Repair Control Transport

**Status:** exploratory candidate schema; equal phase status; unranked; not selected.  
**Lens:** viability/agent  
**Primary claim lane:** A  
**Candidate version:** v1

## One-sentence idea

The transported structure is a viable self-maintaining control package—kernel, endogenous repair law, budgets, and action-credit provenance—carried into a target-native invariant region.

## Formal objects and types

- **Source object:** `CtrlPkg(X_S)=(K_S,f_S,u_S,repair_S,budget_S,credit_S)`
- **Target object:** `CtrlPkg(X_T)`
- **Common comparison type / relation:** A controlled-behavior specification with resource and credit translators.
- **Transported-object atlas IDs:** TO-06;TO-10
- **Invariant IDs:** IV-06
- **Bridge IDs:** BR-03;BR-11
- **Bridge signature:** (B_state,B_control,B_resource,B_credit) forming a forward controlled simulation and carrying K_S into K_T.
- **Bridge morphology:** controlled forward simulation with resource/currency translation and withdrawal gate
- **Bridge data:** viability sets; transition/control laws; repair triggers; budgets; action-credit ledger; disturbance family
- **Admissibility:** Target repair/control is endogenous after withdrawal; margins and resource translations are explicit; public behavior alone is irrelevant.

## Exact and approximate versions

- **Exact preservation:** membership in the viable package region, control-response law, repair semantics, and selected provenance relations
- **Permitted variation:** implementation, redundant resources, and control coordinates within equivalence bounds
- **Approximate/noisy version:** Robust controlled simulation with viability margin m and defect epsilon<m under a named disturbance class.

## Carrier, support, and clocks

- **Source status:** Source control package is stipulated or toy-checkable; no consciousness status.
- **Target status:** Target must have its own control, repair, budgets, and credit minting after withdrawal.
- **Support through stages:** Controls/resources may be shared during transfer but are attributed explicitly; final invariant region uses target resources.
- **Clock/order structure:** Control and repair deadlines use target-local time connected to source stages by a declared schedule relation.

## Preservation audit

- **Hiddenness/public matching:** Viability and repair state can be hidden from public behavior until a disturbance or long-horizon withdrawal test.
- **Action credit/provenance:** Part of the transported object; source-owned or scheduler-owned credit fails the target-native condition.
- **Temporal closure:** Included only as control/repair scheduling needed for maintenance, not the full C6 claim.
- **Recombination/global structure:** Branch and fusion policies must merge budgets/credits explicitly.
- **Viability/maintenance:** Defining component of the schema.

## Route, composition, branching, and deletion

- **Route policy:** Routes spend resources and can alter repair state; route independence requires equal transported budgets/credits or a bounded defect law.
- **Loop/holonomy test:** Run a resource/repair round trip and compare viability margin, ledger, and endogenous control state.
- **Composition:** Compose controlled simulations only if the intermediate viability margin exceeds accumulated defect and currency translators compose.
- **Copy:** Several viable copies can be structural successors; viability does not choose one.
- **Fission:** Branch-valued if each branch inherits an admissible control/repair lineage and viable resources.
- **Fusion:** Fusion-valued only when control conflicts and credit ledgers admit one coherent merged policy.
- **Deletion:** Deletion has no positive force; it may remove source assistance and reveal target failure.
- **Empty target:** Target never enters, or cannot remain in, the declared target viability region after withdrawal.

## Controls and possible later theorem forms

- **Positive control:** S17 endogenous repair using target resources.
- **Negative control:** S28 delayed collapse after bridge withdrawal.
- **Strong adversarial case:** S16 residual-bounded transfer at a viability boundary.
- **Possible theorem shapes:** Robust viability-preservation theorem; autonomy-after-withdrawal theorem; margin composition; external-repair shadow separation.
- **Possible no-go/boundary shapes:** Snapshot or resource sufficiency cannot establish maintenance-law transport; arbitrarily small defects can flip viability at zero margin.
- **Toy discriminators:** two finite controlled systems with translated resources; boundary-crossing residual; endogenous versus external repair; duplicated viable agents.
- **Smallest reconnaissance model:** Two finite controlled transition systems with a 1-bit resource, a repair action, a viability boundary, and an external-controller counterfeit.
- **Minimal dependencies:** finite controlled graphs, reachability/invariance, budget ledgers, exhaustive disturbance checks

## Falsification and kill criteria

- **Direct falsifier/downgrade test:** A claimed margin theorem accepts epsilon>=margin or a target that requires an undeclared external repair/resource channel.
- **Kill criteria:** No nonarbitrary target-native resource boundary; margin cannot compose; control/credit package becomes equivalent to the full unsolved package.
- **Nearest alternative:** TS-09 on discriminators S02;S18;S24;S28.
